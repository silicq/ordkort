Prepositions & adverbs in Norwegian

Prepositions are the hardest part of Norwegian for many learners, because i and på do not follow simple rules: i Oslo but på Lillehammer, i butikken but på jobb. The best way is to learn them with the words they go with. Adverbs of place also come in pairs — hjem / hjemme, ut / ute — for movement and position.

22 words

  • i in
    Vi bor i et hus. We live in a house.
  • på on, at
    Hun er på jobb. She is at work.
  • under under
    Katten er under bordet. The cat is under the table.
  • over over, above
    Lampen henger over bordet. The lamp hangs above the table.
  • foran in front of
    Bilen står foran huset. The car is in front of the house.
  • bak behind
    Hagen er bak huset. The garden is behind the house.
  • mellom between
    Hun satt mellom meg og broren. She sat between me and her brother.
  • ved siden av next to
    Butikken ligger ved siden av banken. The shop is next to the bank.
  • til to
    Vi reiser til Bergen. We are travelling to Bergen.
  • fra from
    Hun kommer fra Kyiv. She comes from Kyiv.
  • med with
    Kaffe med melk, takk. Coffee with milk, please.
  • uten without
    Te uten sukker. Tea without sugar.
  • før before
    Kom før klokka fem. Come before five.
  • etter after
    Vi ses etter jobb. See you after work.
  • hjem home (direction)
    Jeg går hjem nå. I am going home now.
  • hjemme at home
    Er du hjemme? Are you at home?
  • ute outside
    Barna leker ute. The children play outside.
  • inne inside
    Vi er inne når det regner. We stay inside when it rains.
  • der there
    Bor du der? Do you live there?
  • alltid always
    Hun er alltid blid. She is always cheerful.
  • snart soon
    Vi er snart framme. We will be there soon.
  • allerede already
    Toget har allerede gått. The train has already left.
